# windows-cpp: rules, touches and motion

**Status:** Built. How the plain target writes edges, touches, timers, jumps, aim, chase and paths; approach and open questions in [generator](40-generator.md).

- *The edges are looked at before the move.* `edges<Name>()` looks at each edge in the engine's order (top, bottom, left, right) for a thing that is shown and moving, and runs every rule about an edge it is past, in the order written, as the engine runs them; a `<die />` does not cut the rest short (Catch's ball dies and still bounces, as in the engine). What never moves (no velocity, no keys) gets no edge rules, as the engine only looks at the edges for what moves: Breakout's bricks bounce off the sides in the file, but never move. `<bounce />` turns the velocity for the edge (`physics::bounce(ballVelocity, physics::Edge::Top)`); `<stick />` is looked at again after the move, in `stick<Name>()`, as the engine's `keepStuckObjectInBounds`.
- *A projectile is a flag `<fire>` sets.* A `class="projectile"` or `<hidden>` object starts out of play (`chargeAlive = false`), and a `<fire>` (on a key, or in a timer), when it is not out already, places it at the middle of the shooter's top, gives it its own `<velocity>` again and sets the flag, as the engine's `spawnProjectile` takes the first one not flying; from a shooter with a `<facing>`, `physics::fireFrom` places it at the middle of the side faced, just clear, going that way at its own speed (`sf::Vector2f{0.0f, fall1}.length()`). A `<facing>` is fixed when the program is written, so one on something keys move (which turns it) is refused for now. A `<reveal>` is the same flag set again, after `start<Name>()`; a pool fired from a group member's timer is counted with `k`, the member being `i`. From there it is anything that can die: its `<die />` on an edge or a hit takes it out of play until the next shot. A group of projectiles is a pool, the first one out of play fired. The engine's way of keeping it hidden with its collisions off until fired was not copied: one flag says both.
- *A touch is a pair, met in time order.* Every two things shown on a screen that have a rule about each other, one of them able to move, are a `Touch` (`BallBricks`); `move<Screen>()` lists the pairs in the file's order, as the engine's `moveObjects`, leaving out what is out of play or in the air, and `physics::playFrame` meets them earliest first. `touch()` runs the first one's rules about the second, then the second's about the first, as the engine's `applyObjectCollision` does, each with the edge the sweep met (`physics::opposite(edge)` for the first). A rule that turns a velocity uses that edge (`physics::bounce(ballVelocity, edge)`), so nothing is put back by a guess.
- *`<ride />` is a velocity the rules set.* An object that rides has `frogRiding`, cleared at the start of `move<Screen>()`, set by a touch to what it touches' velocity (the last one wins, as in the engine), and moved by with its own velocity for the rest of the frame (`frog.move((frogVelocity + frogRiding) * fraction)`). A reset clears it. A group's members do not ride yet.
- *`unless=` is a function per class.* Each class an `unless=` names gets `template <typename Shape> bool touchingLogs(const Shape& one, const void* other)`, which goes through everything of that class shown with what asks (in play, for what can die) and leaves out `one` and the `other` of the touch, as `Game::isTouchingClass` does; the rule's `if` asks `!touchingLogs(frog, &water)`, or with `nullptr` on an edge. A template, so one function serves a shape, a sprite and a text, as in `physics.h`; checking the class's things inline in each rule was the other way, and repeats the loops in every rule that asks. Like the touches, it is plain overlap, so a thing exactly edge to edge with a log is not on it in the program, and is in the engine.
- *A timer is a count of frames.* Each `<timer>` is an `int` (a `std::vector<int>` for a group, one for each member, as the engine gives each member its own): `bomber1Timer2`, the frames until it goes off. At 0 it is worked out again (`framesFor(drop1)`, the engine's rounding, a `<random>` drawn anew); when the count gets back to 0 its commands run, and an `<after>` sets it to -1, done. An object's timers are `tick<Name>()`, called by each screen that shows it before anything moves, as the engine runs the timers first, and only while it is in play; a screen's are written in its update after them, and one that changes the screen ends the frame. Counting seconds with `sf::Clock` was the other way, and would not match the engine's frames.
- *A touch only where both are shown.* The engine touches only what the screen showing shows, so each screen's `move<Screen>()` lists only the pairs it shows both of (Megamania's laser is on every wave, each wave's ships on one).
- *A heading turns the picture.* An object with a `<heading>` has `shipHeading` and `turnShip()`, which keeps it from 0 up to 360 and, when the whole degree changes, draws its picture again at it (the engine's own `xge::rasterizeTurned`, or `xge::turnBitmap` of its rows, a square the drawing turns in) and keeps those pixels for its touches; a `<turn>` held changes the heading and calls it, `<thrust>` adds `physics::ahead(heading) * amount` to the velocity, and a `<fire>` leaves the nose along the heading at the shot's own speed (`physics::fireAhead`). Turning the `sf::Sprite` with `setRotation` was the other way, and was not chosen: the engine's picture is redrawn, square, at whole degrees, and its touches test that picture, so the program would see other pixels. Only one sprite of lines or rows, no `<flip>`, can have a heading so far.
- *A pull and drag are lines in `accelerate<Name>()`.* `<acceleration>` is `landerAcceleration`, added to the velocity before the edges and the move, cleared by a `<stop />` and put back by a reset; `<drag>` is `shipVelocity *= 1.0f - drag;`, after it, as the engine's `applyAcceleration`. An `<accelerate>` held adds to the velocity one way, and one with `burn=` only while that variable is above 0, taking 1 from it each frame. A held action is one `if` over all its keys, so two keys of one action held at once do it once, as the engine holds an action, not a key.
- *Pixel touches are swept.* A thing of `<type>pixel</type>` keeps its picture's pixels (`landerPixels`; `pixelsOf(texture)` for a group of several pictures), and `physics::sweepPixels` finds the moment two things first touch by their pixels, as the engine's `firstPixelHit` does: an Asteroids shot moves 8 pixels a frame through lines 2 thick. Everything moves only to that moment before the rules run. A rule with `<slower>` or `<faster>` compares the speed taken once as the touch begins (`landerSpeed`), as the engine does, so a `<stop />` in one does not change which of the next run; against a group, where that is one speed per member, it is refused for now.
- *A `<jump>` is a step and a count.* A jumper has `baileyJumpStep` and `baileyJumpFrames`; `physics::jump` starts one (the distance over `framesFor(seconds)`) unless one is under way or it would land off the window, as the engine's `startJump`, and `physics::jumping`, at the start of `move<Screen>()`, takes the next step if it stays inside and counts down. While the count is above 0 its touches are left out, on both sides and in `unless=` functions, as the engine touches nothing in the air. A reset ends the jump.
- *An action is the same code wherever it is set off.* A pressed action is one block (`trigger-action`), written again for a `<trigger>` in a condition or timer, so a `<reset />` in an action (`start<Name>()` of its object) or a `<play>` there runs the same either way. When a touch has both sides' rules and one has its own guard (`unless=`, `sprite=`), the guard is an `if` around that side's commands only, so it does not hold back the other side's.
- *Walking, falling, landing and climbing are the engine's, frame by frame.* Something keys move has the steps its held keys add up to (`manSteps`, cleared at the top of the screen's update and added to by each held `<move>`), and `physics::steer` sets that way of its velocity only when the steps change, as the engine's key events set it when a key is pressed or let go (and not across while it is in a leap). `<land />` is `physics::land` with the side the sweep met, the engine's `landOn`: on a top it is put on it and its fall stopped. `manGrounded` is cleared at the start of the move and set by a landing, so a `<leap>` (`-sqrt(2 * pull * height)` up, as the engine's) goes only from the ground. `<climb>` is `physics::climb` with `ladderAt(man)`, a function per ladder class like the `unless=` ones, which looks only at what the screen showing shows; on a ladder nothing pulls it and its velocity is 0. A group's `<acceleration>` is one pull for every member, each with its own velocity (Donkey Kong's barrels). A leap that starts in the frame its move key is first held goes straight up: the walk is read once a frame, the engine's key event comes first.
- *Aim and chase look for the nearest in play by a function.* What an `<aim>` or `<chase>` names gets `nearest<Name>()`, the middle of the nearest of it in play (alive, and shown on this screen), or none; a group's takes the one looking, an object's nothing. `physics::chase` sets the velocity straight at it (still within `<near>`, as it was with none); `physics::aimAt` gives the way, kept in `baseAim` with `baseAimed` until the thing starts again, and a `<fire>` from something aimed goes along it (`physics::fireAlong`) before a heading or a facing would. Looking the nearest one up where it is needed was chosen over a list of everything in play kept each frame, which the program would carry for two verbs. An aim or a chase turns the `<facing>` too (`physics::facingOf`, the axis it goes further along), and so does a key that moves, hops or jumps the thing when it is pressed, as the engine's do; what can turn keeps `playerFacing`, set back only by a whole start. An aim also turns the engine's `<heading>`, which the program does not, so `<aim>` on something with a heading is refused.
- *A path is a row in a table, and flying it is the engine's, frame by frame.* `<paths>` become `enum class Path` and `routes`, a `physics::Route` for each (speed, start, legs of a step or home), and what follows keeps a `physics::Flight<Path>` and a home (`flagshipsFlight[i]`, `flagshipsHome[i]`); `fly<Name>()`, after the timers and before anything moves, calls `physics::fly`, which is `Game::applyPaths` over one thing. What a step does as it begins (a sound, a bomb) is the follower's own, so it is a lambda in `fly<Name>()` with an `if` for each such leg, written as the follower's timer would write it. A path written out as code for each follower (a `switch` on the leg) was the other way, and was not chosen: the legs are data, and Galaxian's five groups would each carry the dive. Home is where the thing stood at its whole start, as the engine's `positionOriginal` (a `<reset />` alone, which draws a `<random>` start again, leaves it).
- *A circle touches as a circle.* `physics::touching` tests a circle against a box by the box's nearest point and two circles by their middles, as the engine's `circleRectangle` does; boxes alone overlap by more than an edge.
- *`<release>` is `<reveal>` from where it is.* The first of the pool out of play (as many as it says), started again and centred on the thing whose rule or timer releases them.
- *A label placed by a number's size follows it.* A `<number>` text is re-measured and re-placed by `show<Name>()` whenever its variable changes; any object whose `<position>` names that number's `.width` or `.height` (Lunar Lander's FUEL label) is re-placed in the same function, as the engine re-places it when the size changes. Placing it once at start left it at the width of an empty text.
