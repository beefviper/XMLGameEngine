# Sound

**Status:** Built (`sound.h`, `audio.h`, `audio_*.cpp`).

## What we wanted

The 8-bit machines these games come from played bleeps, bloops and little tunes from a sound chip, not recordings. A sound written as notes in the game file fits the format the way `<bitmap>` does: part of the description, readable, diffable, no file beside it.

## The language

A game has an optional `<sounds>` after `<variables>`. Each `<sound name wave>` has an optional `<volume>` (0 to 1, a value, default 0.3), then `<note>`s and `<rest>`s in order. `<note pitch to wave>seconds</note>`: `pitch` is a note name with octave (`C4` is middle C, `A4` 440 Hz, `F#3`, `Bb5`) or hertz; `to` slides evenly in semitones; `wave` is `square` (default), `triangle`, `sawtooth`, `sine`, `noise`. `<play sound="name" />` is an ordinary command (collision, action, key, condition, timer). An optional `<loop />` first in a `<sound>` makes it play over and over until `<silence sound="name" />`, a reset of the game, or the release of the key whose `<play>` started it. Length and volume are values (a variable can set the tempo); `pitch`, `to` and `wave` pick something, so they are attributes ([vision](01-vision.md)).

## Decisions

- **Notes, not sound files.** Files need a loader per library, shipping, and are opaque in the XML. A file tag can come later beside notes.
- **The engine makes the samples; a backend only plays them.** `synthesize()` (`sound.cpp`) produces 16-bit mono at 44100 Hz, the same for every library; a backend hands them over (`sf::SoundBuffer`, raylib `LoadSoundFromWave`, an SDL audio callback with a small mixer of its own). Each library's own synthesis does not exist in SFML or SDL2, and would make a sound depend on the library. The synthesizer is tested with no sound device.
- **Waves of a chip:** square, triangle, sawtooth tone channels; noise as percussion, pitched like a chip (a new random level every half cycle, seeded per note, so it sounds the same every time and in every library); sine kept as a soft option. Plucked string, piano and horn from the author's waveform tester were left out as not of the era.
- **Slides by semitones** (exponential in hertz) so a bend sounds even; phase accumulates per sample.
- **Looping is the sound's, holding is the key's (built 2026-10-14).** A thrust hum or a piece of music is made to loop, so `<loop />` sits on the `<sound>`, an empty element like `<wrap />`, not a flag on each `<play>`. A key's `<play>` of a loop is held like a `<move>`: on with the press, off with the release, off when a state change lets go of the keys, on again when the new state's held keys resume (`executeHeldInput`). Anywhere else a `<play>` switches it on and `<silence>` off, the verb also stopping a sound that plays once. A loop is state, not an event: `Game` keeps the set that is on (`loopingSounds`), and `Engine` compares it each frame with what it told the `Audio`, so a frame that silences and asks again (one of two keys for thrust let go) does not restart it, and `Engine::silence()` (xgegui's pause) only has to forget what was playing. Each library loops in its own way: SFML's `setLooping`, a wrap in the SDL mixer, and in raylib a `Music` stream (a `Sound` cannot loop) read from the samples written out as a WAV in memory and fed each frame by `Audio::update()`. `<silence>` was chosen over `<stop>` (an object's verb) and `<hush>`. Lunar Lander's main engine and side jets and Asteroids' engine use it; Lunar Lander silences both when the fuel runs out.
- **Music (built 2026-10-16): parts, and a state's own sound.** Two things stood between the sounds and music. (1) **Parts**: a `<sound>` can be written as `<part wave>`s, each its notes and rests in order, all playing at once, as a chip's channels did: a tune over a bass, or a chord as parts of one note. A part's `wave` is its notes' default, between the sound's and the note's own. The synthesizer makes each part from the start and adds them, the sound as long as its longest part, a sum past full scale cut off rather than wrapped; there is no automatic scaling, so one part sounds as it did before and `<volume>` stays the author's. Each note carries its part number (`SoundNote::part`), so the flat list, the generator and every backend stayed as they were. A sound has parts or loose notes, not both, so nothing is ambiguous about which part a loose note is in. (2) **`<music sound>` on a state**: a loop is on while the state is the current one, so play has background music that stops under a pause with nothing written on the pause key; a sound that plays once plays as the state begins (a push, or `<pop state>` switching to it), not when the state over it is taken off, so an ending's tune is the ending's and the condition that pushes it does not have to remember it. `Game::soundsOn()` is the loops on plus the current state's music, which Engine compares each frame with what is playing. Pac-Man shows both: a siren under play, two-part tunes at the start and the endings. Looked at and set aside: a `<music>` element of its own beside `<sounds>` (a tune is a sound; one list is simpler), tempo and note lengths in beats (a length is already a value, so `beat / 2` works), a tracker-style pattern table (a little language), automatic mixing down by the number of parts, a state's music carrying on through a pause from where it was (each library would have to pause one sound; restarting is what the old machines mostly did). Open: a volume envelope (decay, for plucked and bell sounds); silencing a state's music while it is current; Invaders' march (a sequence stepped by a timer).
- **A short fade (about 2 ms), no envelope.** An attack/decay/release would be a child of `<sound>` or `<note>`.
- **One voice per sound,** like a chip channel: replaying restarts it, different sounds overlap. A sound asked for twice in one frame plays once. A voice pool would let a sound overlap itself; not needed.
- **The game asks, the engine plays.** `<play>` queues a name (`Game::requestSound`); `Engine` owns the `Audio` and plays the queue at the end of `step()`. `Game` never touches audio, so the tests drive whole games with no device and read the requests (`takeSoundRequests()`). `Engine(game, window)` with no audio is `NullAudio`.
- **Checked at load,** with messages that say where: unusable wave, pitch or length, volume outside 0 to 1, a sound with no notes, duplicate names, `<play>` naming no sound. A note or rest is at most 10 seconds.
- **The sound library is its own choice** (`AudioBackend`: SFML3, Raylib, SDL2, None; `-a`/`--audio`, an Options entry), not tied to the window, so an OpenGL window (GLFW has no sound) can have sound, and `-a none` plays silently. Default SFML 3.
- **No sound is not an error:** a window that will not open stops the game; a sound device that will not open prints a warning and uses `NullAudio` (xgegui says so and sets the choice to None).
- **SDL is shared** by `SDL2Window` and `SDL2Audio`: each starts and stops only its own subsystem (`SDL_InitSubSystem`/`QuitSubSystem`), the last one out calls `SDL_Quit()`. raylib opens its sound device apart from its window. SFML 3 and raylib both carry miniaudio and `stb_image` (link clashes when both are built static from source; [backends](50-backends.md)).
- **Pong was the test:** low square blip (A3) off walls, higher (A4) off a paddle, a falling triangle bloop for a point, a rising arpeggio on start, a falling four-note tune with a hiss of noise when someone reaches 15. Since then Space Invaders (both), Kaboom, Demon Attack, Frostbite and Berserk have sounds too (shots, hits, explosions, jumps, waves, game over). Breakout, Frogger, Space Race, Freeway, Depth Charge, Astrosmash, Lunar Lander and Asteroids have them now, in the same few voices: a low square blip off a wall and a higher one off a paddle, noise that falls for anything that explodes, splashes or crunches (the bigger the rock in Asteroids, the lower), a short falling zap for a shot, a rising arpeggio on start, the same six-note tune for a win and a falling triangle tune for game over. A shot's sound sits beside its `<fire>` in the action, as in Space Invaders; the end sounds sit in the condition that pushes the end screen, as in Kaboom.

## Rejected

Sound files as the only form; a tracker or MML string (`"t120 o4 c8 e8 g8"`: a little language inside a string, which [vision](01-vision.md) took out); each library's own synthesis; a voice pool; sound tied to the window library.

## Not done

A volume envelope, stereo/panning, a sound-file tag, notes that follow a variable at runtime (a sound is made once when the window opens). Invaders' march is still silent: it is not a loop but one of four notes per step of the fleet, faster as it thins, which needs the next note of a sequence each time (a timer playing the sounds in turn). The loop's end meets its start at the 2 ms fades, a faint pulse once a loop; unheard by agents. A shot's sound is asked for when the key is pressed, even if the gun is not ready and no shot leaves (Depth Charge, Astrosmash, Asteroids, as in Space Invaders).
